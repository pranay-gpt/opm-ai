CO2SOL

CO2SOL is a three-phase compositional option for simulating CO2 flooding in oil reservoirs (EOR, storage in oil depleted reservoirs). Water is not allowed to dissolve in the gas or oil phases, CO2 can be present in the three phases, and other components in the oil and gas phases.

See "CO2/H2S solution in the aqueous phase".

CO2 partitioning between the oil and gas phases is calculated by a fugacity equilibration method. A cubic equation of state is used to model oil and gas phase densities and fugacities.

The amount of CO2 dissolved in water, and other aqueous phase properties, are computed using solubility data that you enter with either the SOLUBILI, SOLUBILS or SOLUBILT keywords or defaulted following Chang et al, 1996 . When using SOLUBILI or SOLUBILT the salting-out effect is taken into account by correcting solubility in pure water with the Chang et al correlation for brine (Chang et al, 1996) . The SOLUBILS keyword can be used to describe a more complex salt dependency using the BRINE tracking. The SOLUBILT keyword can be used to describe a more complex temperature dependency. To obtain details on the solubility data used in a simulation the SOLU mnemonic may be specified in the RPTPROPS keyword.

Molecular diffusion between gases can be modeled using the DIFFUSE option. See also "Diffusion".

It is possible to set initial dissolved CO2 as a function of depth using the RSWVD or RSW keywords. This initial solution honors this value unless it exceeds the saturated CO2 concentration in which case the saturated value is used.

Notes

  • The initial CO2 concentration may not be in exact equilibrium with the hydrocarbon phase. In such a case the initial flash slightly modifies the input values.
  • The first line of the solubility table defined using the SOLUBILI keyword represents water at standard conditions, when the dissolved CO2 amount is at a minimum. The simulator does not assume that the amount of CO2 in water at the lowest pressure is zero; if you want to specify it as such then you should set it to zero in the first line of the table.
  • Instantaneous equilibrium is assumed between the oil, gas and water phases.
