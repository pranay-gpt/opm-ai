Geological storage

In Aquifers
Using the CO2STORE option, three phases are considered: a CO2-rich phase (labeled gas phase), an H2O-rich phase (labeled liquid phase) and a solid phase.

This option gives accurate mutual solubilities of CO2 in water (xCO2) and water in the CO2-rich phase (yH2O). Salts are described as components of the liquid/solid phase. To allow for a solid phase the SOLID option should be used.

The GASWAT option provides a gas/aqueous phase equilibrium method. The liquid mole fraction x CO2 is accurately predicted; however, the gas phase mole fraction yH2O is less accurate. The gas composition is not restricted to CO2/H2O, as other gases and their solubility in water can be included.

In Oil Depleted Reservoirs and EOR (Enhanced oil recovery)
The option best suited for this case is CO2SOL. Water is not considered in the oil or gas phase, but the solubility of CO2 in the water phase is accounted for. The gas/oil composition is not restricted to CO2 but only CO2 is considered soluble in water.
In Gas Depleted Reservoirs
This case can be modeled using the GASWAT option by adding gas components, such as CH4 and H2S for example.
ECBM
The COAL option provides a diffusive flow and sorption model for Enhanced Coal Bed Methane recovery (ECBM). A full description of this option is provided in "Coal bed methane model".

Since the CO2STORE, CO2SOL and GASWAT options estimate the fluid properties differently, they cannot be used together.

To model depleted reservoirs together with the THERMAL option, the CO2STORE keyword should be used with the required hydrocarbon components. A full description of this option is provided in "Thermal CO2STORE".
