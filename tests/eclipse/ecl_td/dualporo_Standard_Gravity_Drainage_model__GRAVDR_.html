Standard gravity drainage model (GRAVDR)

The illustration shows a typical block of matrix material containing oil and water. The fractional height of the water table in the fracture is X W and the fractional height of the water displacement front in the block of matrix material is X w.




Figure 78.1 A typical block of matrix material containing oil and water

The pressure difference due to gravity alone is

Δ P=D Zmat (XW -Xw )(ρ w-ρ o)g [Eq. 78.1]

where

ρ w is the density of water
ρ o is the density of oil at reservoir conditions.

DZ mat is the same as lz in Kazemi's formula.

Integrating Δ P over all blocks of matrix material within a reservoir simulation cell leads to the same formula for Δ P, except that it now represents the gravitational pressure difference between reservoir simulation fracture and matrix cells, and X W and Xw are the partial volumes of mobile water in the simulation fracture and matrix cells. In ECLIPSE, the effect is modeled by introducing additional pseudo capillary pressures, P S

PSco w=DZmat ⋅X W( ρo -ρ w) g (fracture cells)

PSco w=DZmat ⋅X w( ρo -ρ w) g (matrix cells)

PSco g=DZmat ⋅X G( ρo -ρ g) g (fracture cells)

PSco g=DZmat ⋅X g( ρo -ρ g) g (matrix cells)

PScw g=DZmat ⋅X G( ρw -ρ g) g (fracture cells)

PScw g=DZmat ⋅X g( ρw -ρ g) g (matrix cells)

Thus, the flow of oil and gas from a fracture to a matrix cell in a gas oil system is computed as

Fg⁢=  TR ⋅GMOB ×(Pof -P om+ df mρ gg +Pcogf -P cogm + DZmat(X G- Xg )(ρo -ρg)g 2) [Eq. 78.2]
Fo= TR⋅ OMOB (P of- Pom+ dfm ρ og- DZmat( XG -X g)(ρo-ρg )g2 ) [Eq. 78.3]

where

TR is the transmissibility between the fracture and matrix cells,
GMOB is the gas mobility in the (upstream) fracture cell,
OMOB is the oil mobility in the (upstream) fracture cell,
P of is the oil phase pressure in the fracture cell,
P om is the oil phase pressure in the matrix cell,
d fm is the difference in depth between the fracture and matrix cells (usually zero),
ρ g is the density of gas at reservoir conditions,
ρ o is the density of oil at reservoir conditions,
g is the acceleration due to gravity,
P cogf is the capillary pressure of gas in the fracture cell (normally zero)
P cogm is the capillary pressure of gas in the matrix cell.
Note: The gravity drainage head DZmat(XG-Xg)(ρo-ρg)g has been split between the two phases.

In dual porosity / dual permeability models in which the gravity imbibition / drainage mechanism is active, the calculation of the initial reservoir state (see "Initializing the study") occurs independently of the value of DZm at that is the reservoir is initialized without regard for the gravity imbibition / drainage forces acting between the matrix and fracture cells. When the simulation begins, the gravity imbibition / drainage forces may cause fluid movement between matrix cells and their corresponding fracture cells due to the different fluid levels in the matrix / fracture cells. If the redistribution of fluids causes a significant transient when the simulation is started, you can overcome this by activating item 11 of the OPTIONS keyword. If this switch is activated, the effect is to cause modifications to the phase pressures to make the initial solution a true steady state. These phase pressure modifications are applied for the duration of the run.

ECLIPSE 300

In ECLIPSE 300, a different value of the σ coupling may be specified for gravity drainage by using the SIGMAGD keyword. If the values entered with SIGMA and SIGMAGD, [denoted σ and σ gd respectively] differ, the program uses an interpolated σ value for each phase which depends on the relative strengths of the capillary pressure and gravity drainage terms:

σp eff =f p⋅σ+(1- fp )⋅σ gd [Eq. 78.4]

where p =oil, gas, water, and

fp=|Δ Pp || ΔP p|+ |ΔP pgd | [Eq. 78.5]

where

( Ppi ,Ppj ) are the cell pressures,
( Ppci ,Ppcj) are the cell capillary pressures,
