Viscosity model

The effective oil and miscible gas viscosities follow the Todd-Longstaff model. They are given by:

μ o eff =μo 1-ω μmω [Eq. 396.1]
μ g eff =μg 1-ω μmω [Eq. 396.2]

The mixture viscosity μ m is given by a 1/4-power fluid mixing rule

1μm 1/4 = Sg ′ Sn ′ (1 μg ) 1/4 +S o′ S n′ ( 1μo )1/ 4 [Eq. 396.3]

which can be rewritten as

μm=μ oμ g ( Sg ′ Sn ′⋅μo1/4 + So′ Sn′⋅ μg 1/4 )4 [Eq. 396.4]

where the mobile saturations are defined by:

So′=So-So r

Sg′=Sg-Sg c

Sn′= So′+ Sg′

For a fully mixed state, that is ω=1, the effective viscosities are equal to the mixture viscosities, μ o eff =μg eff=μm. This case models a large dispersed oil-gas zone. The Todd-Longstaff model treats this case as a local unit mobility ratio displacement.

For a fully segregated case, that is ω=0, the effective viscosities are equal to the ‘pure’ phase viscosities, μ o eff =μo, μ g eff =μg. Such a case corresponds to a local high adverse mobility ratio displacement and models a negligibly thin oil-gas dispersed zone.

The mixing parameter approach allows the case of a partial mixing zone to be modeled by choosing an intermediate value of ω.
