Non-Darcy flow

x ECLIPSE 100
x ECLIPSE 300

The non-Darcy feature described here is the Forchheimer correction which takes into account the inertia effects due to high velocity that may occur in high permeability regions, such as fractures. The Forchheimer parameter can be input by using either the VDFLOW or VDFLOWR keywords in the PROPS section. For a homogeneous reservoir the model writes (cgs units):

∂P∂x  =(μ Kk rA )q+ βρ( qA )2 [Eq. 728.1]

where:

q is the volumetric flow rate
K is the rock permeability
kr is the relative permeability
A is the area through which flow occurs
μ is the dynamic fluid viscosity
ρ is the fluid density
β is the Forchheimer parameter
∂P∂x  is the pressure gradient normal to the area

The Forchheimer parameter β is the user input in Forchheimer units: 1 Forch = 1 atm.s2.gm-1. An alternative unit for β can be obtained by noting that 1 atm = 1.01325 bars and 1 bar = 106 gm.cm-1.s-2, therefore 1 Forch = 1 atm.s2.gm-1 = 1.01325.106 cm-1.

Typical values in cm-1 are rather large for engineering purposes and the Forchheimer (Forch) is used in all unit sets. Dake (1978) shows a typical value of β =10 7cm −1 =9.86 Forch at K= 60 mD.

For other unit sets (FIELD, METRIC, LAB and PVT) two constants C 1 and C 2 are introduced as

∂P∂x  =1 C1 (μ Kk rA )q+ C2C1 βρ(qA )2 [Eq. 728.2]

When considering the flow between two grid blocks i and n the simulator use transmissibility. Applying block-centered transmissibility for simplicity (OLDTRAN) gives:

q =C1 FN DT in krμ ΔPin [Eq. 728.3]

where:

Tin=A ΔXi2Ki+ΔX n2 Kn [Eq. 728.4]

and where the non-Darcy flow factor is given by:

FND =1 1+C2ρ T ink r A2 μ1 2( βi ΔX i+ βn ΔX n)q [Eq. 728.5]

Note that the FND factor is dimensionless and gives a quantitative indication of the deviation of the flow behavior from Darcy’s law for which FND=1 .

Construction of flows

From the equation for FND there are two coupled equations to solve for each phase p:

FND =1 1+C 2ρp Ti nA2 k rp μp 1 2( βi ΔX i+ βn ΔXn)q [Eq. 728.8]

and

q= C1 FND Tin (k rp μp)ΔPp in [Eq. 728.9]

Substituting q into F ND a quadratic equation is obtained for FND

BFND 2+ FND-1 =0 [Eq. 728.10]

that can be solved to give (the negative root being ignored)

F ND= -1+1+4 B2 B [Eq. 728.11]

B =C 2C 1ρ p(T ni A)2( kr pμp )2 12 (βiΔ Xi +β nΔ Xn )Δ Ppni [Eq. 728.12]

Assumption and simplifications

As the Forchheimer correction is expected to be significant only in regions of high velocity, it is assumed that those regions have a constant rock permeability K and constant porosity φ. Under these assumptions the B -factor can be simplified to:

B =C 2C 1β ρp (T niK A) ( krp μ p )2 ΔP pni [Eq. 728.13]

Using the fact that in ECLIPSE the factor unit C 1 is included in the transmissibility (T ni= C1Tni ), the final form for the B -factor used in ECLIPSE 300 is obtained:

B =C 2βρp(T niK A) ( krp μ p) 2ΔP pni [Eq. 728.14]

The values of F ND and B/ (ΔPpni) for gas and oil may be examined using the mnemonics FFOR (G, O) and BFOR (G, O) respectively in the RPTSOL or RPTSCHED keywords and also as summary vectors using the mnemonics BFFOR (G, O) and BBFOR (G, O) respectively.

Note: In the current version, ECLIPSE 100 reports FFORG and BFORG, that is, for the gas phase only. Summary vectors are not available.
