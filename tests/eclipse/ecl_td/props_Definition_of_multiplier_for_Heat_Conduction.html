Definition of multiplier for Heat Conduction

Option 1: using THCONR

Thermal transmissibilities can be multiplied by a saturation dependent factor Λ. Following Coats (1980) , it is assumed that the thermal transmissibility is a function of the gas saturation, Sg, only. This method does not distinguish between the thermal transmissibility of a rock filled with oil and the thermal transmissibility of a rock filled with water.

The multiplier Λ is defined to be:

Λ= (1-α Sg) [Eq. 325.1]

where α is specified by the THCONSF keyword.

From the definition of THCONR above, a value for α can be determined as:

α=θ⋅( Kl -K g)/THCONR

To check these values, we can calculate the conductivities for a liquid filled cell S g=0 and a gas filled cell S g=1:

THCONR ⋅Λ(0)=θ⋅ Kl +(1- θ)⋅ Kr

THCONR ⋅Λ(1)=θ⋅ Kg +(1- θ)⋅ Kr

which are just the porosity weighted averages of the rock and fluid conductivities

Option 2: using THCROCK

Thermal transmissibilities are multiplied by a saturation dependent factor Λ. For this option, Λ takes the form:

Λ= (φ(ΣpS pKp)+ (1-φ )Krock )/THCAVE

In the above expression, φ is the porosity; THCOIL is denoted by K oil, THCGAS is denoted by K gas, THCWATER is denoted by K water, THCSOLID is denoted by K solid and THCROCK, specifying the rock thermal conductivity, is denoted by Krock. The phase saturations are represented by Sp.

For a perfectly homogeneous reservoir, with THCAV Ei=THC AVEj , the T HCAVE term in the Λ formula will cancel the T HCAVE term in the thermal transmissibilities, and the heat flow will be proportional to (φ( Σp SpK p)+(1 -φ) Krock ). However, for heterogeneous reservoirs, the T HCAVE terms will not cancel out, since the transmissibility uses a harmonic average of neighbor cell T HCAVE values, while the Λ formula uses the upstream cell T HCAVE value.
