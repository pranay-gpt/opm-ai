Conversion from moles to equivalent mass

In the output at the end of the PRT file, in addition to the net carbon moles produced or injected, the 'equivalent mass of carbon-12' and the 'equivalent mass of CO2' are also reported. These are calculated as follows:

Equivalent mass of carbon-12

Equivalent mass of carbon-12 [lb] = (moles of carbon [lb-mole]) * (molecular weight of carbon-12 [lb/lb-mole])

where the molecular weight of carbon-12 is 12 in all unit systems (by definition). The resulting value is then output in the appropriate unit for the unit system used in the model.

Equivalent mass of CO2

Because each mole of CO2 contains one mole of carbon atoms, the total amount of CO2 produced by burning the net carbon molar production in the presence of sufficient oxygen is:

Equivalent mass of CO2 [lb] = (moles of carbon [lb-mole]) * (molecular weight of CO2 [lb/lb-mole])

where the molecular weight of CO2 is 44.01 in all unit systems. As above, the resulting value is then output in the appropriate unit for the unit system used in the model.

Note: The 'equivalent mass of CO2' reported here is different to the similarly named 'CO2 equivalent', which is commonly used in carbon accounting literature. CO2 equivalent is a measure of how much a gas contributes to global warming, relative to carbon dioxide, and is calculated based on the mass of the gas (in tonnes) multiplied by the published global warming potential (GWP) of the gas. Such a value does not easily lend itself to the reservoir simulation system where much of the carbon produced is in the liquid phase.
