Miscibility and surface tension effects

ECLIPSE 100
x ECLIPSE 300

The surface tension between the oil and gas phases can be used as a measure of how miscible the two fluids are. The ECLIPSE 300 MISCIBLE model follows that of Coats (1979) , where parameters that are functions of surface tension are used to interpolate properties such as relative permeability and capillary pressure between immiscible and miscible values:

Kro =F k⋅ Kro imm +(1 -Fk)⋅ Kromis [Eq. 172.1]
Krg =F k⋅ Krg imm +(1 -Fk)⋅ Krgmis [Eq. 172.2]
Pco g=Fp⋅ Pcogim m [Eq. 172.3]

Kroimm , Krg imm and Pcog imm are the immiscible oil and gas relative permeabilities and capillary pressure obtained from the user-defined rock curves (for example SGFN).

The miscible capillary pressure is taken to be zero.

Kromis and Krg mis are the miscible oil and gas relative permeabilities. These can be specified as user-defined rock curves using the MISCNUM keyword. If these are not specified, then by default they are taken to be straight lines. These straight line relative permeability curves connect the zero point to the maximum point on the immiscible curve. That is the miscible relative permeabilities are defined as

Kro mis =K ro,max⁡imm ( So S o,max⁡ imm ) [Eq. 172.4]
Krg mis =K rg,max⁡imm ( Sg S g,max⁡ imm ) [Eq. 172.5]

where So,max⁡i mm and Sg,max⁡i mm are the maximum saturations on the immiscible oil and gas relative permeability curves respectively and Kro, max⁡imm and Krg, max⁡imm are the corresponding maximum relative permeability values. This implies that Kro,max⁡ mis=Kr o,max⁡ imm and Krg, max⁡mis= Krg ,max⁡imm .

The interpolation parameters F k and F p are functions of the surface tension σ between the oil and gas phases. For high surface tension the parameters tend to 1 and the flow becomes is immiscible. For low surface tension the parameters tend to 0 and the flow becomes miscible.

In order to add the relative permeability curves sensibly, the critical saturations must also be scaled so that both the immiscible and miscible relative permeabilities have the same endpoints:

Soc r=Fk⋅ Socrim m+( 1-F k) ⋅S ocr mis [Eq. 172.6]
Sgc r=Fk⋅ Sgcrim m+( 1-F k) ⋅S gcr mis [Eq. 172.7]

Socr imm and S gcr imm are the critical immiscible saturations, generally obtained from the user-defined rock curves, though in the case of hysteresis they may be the critical point of a scanning curve (see "Hysteresis" for further information).

The critical saturations for miscible flow S ocr mis and Sgcr mis are by default zero (unless the MISCNUM keyword is used), so these equations simplify to:

Soc r=Fk⋅ Socrim m [Eq. 172.8]
Sgc r=Fk⋅ Sgcrim m [Eq. 172.9]

The relative permeabilities defined by equations 172.1 and 172.2 are two phase (oil-gas) relative permeabilities. When there are three phases present, these values are used to calculate three phase relative permeabilities, as described in "Saturation Functions".

Note the similarity between this model and that provided by the Velocity Dependent Relative Permeability option: see "Velocity-dependent relative permeabilities". Note also that the length of timestepping is of great importance in simulating surface tension effects.
