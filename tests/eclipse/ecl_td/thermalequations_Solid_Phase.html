Solid phase

Solid components can be used in thermal simulations. A solid component cannot also appear in the liquid or gas phase, but it can be created by chemical reactions, see "Chemical reactions". If solid components are modeled, then a number of modifications are required to the formulation described above.

The volume balance residual equation 310.3 is modified to include the solid volume V s:

Rv=V p-V f-Vs =0

The total fluid molar density m t is still calculated by equation 311.2, but the sum is taken over the fluid components only.

The energy density equation, 313.4, is modified to include the solid energy e s:

Vb ⋅e= Vp⋅mt ⋅(L⋅eo +V⋅ eg +W⋅e w)+Vp ⋅e s+( Vb -V p)⋅er

Equation 314.2, which is derived from the volume balance equation, is replaced by

Rv =V p- Vp⋅(V o+Vg+ Vw +V sol)=0

where V sol is the specific solid volume.

The equations for the saturations, equation 314.3 are modified to include the solid volume:

S^o =V o Vo +V g+Vw+ Vsol=V f Vf +Vsol ⋅S o

S^g =V g Vo +V g+Vw+ Vsol=V f Vf +Vsol ⋅S g

S^ w =V w Vo +Vg+V w+ Vso l= VfVf+ Vsol⋅Sw

S^so l= Vso lVo+ Vg +V w+Vsol

The modified saturations satisfy

S^o +S ^ g+ S^ w+ S^ sol=1

Equation 314.3 is still used to define normalized saturations S o, Sg and S w, representing the fraction of the fluid volume that is in each fluid phase. The normalized saturations satisfy

So+S g+Sw =1

The normalized saturations are used in calculating the relative permeabilities in equations 315.3 and 315.4.
