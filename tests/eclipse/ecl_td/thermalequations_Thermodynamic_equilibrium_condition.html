Thermodynamic equilibrium condition

The phase properties used in the residual and Jacobian equations (that is the fluid volume and the flow terms) can be calculated from the component properties once the mole fractions of each component in each phase have been determined.

The number of moles of each component (including water) can be written as

mc =mt⋅L⋅ xo c+ mt ⋅V⋅ xgc+mt⋅ W⋅x wc [Eq. 311.1]

where

mt ⋅L⋅xoc , mt ⋅V⋅ xgc and mt⋅W⋅x wc are the number of moles in the oil, vapor and water phases.

mt is the total fluid molar density given by

mt=∑c mc +mw [Eq. 311.2]

L, V and W are the oil, vapor and water mole fractions and

xpc are the component phase fractions.

Two simplifying conditions are imposed, that there is no water in the oil phase, and no hydrocarbon in the water phase:

xw c=0, c=1, …,N

xw w=1,

if W> 0

It is also assumed the three phases are in thermodynamic equilibrium, determined by user-defined K-values:

xgc= Kc⁡(P,T )⋅xoc [Eq. 311.3]
xgw= Kw⁡(P,T) ⋅x ww [Eq. 311.4]

The K-values are functions of pressure P and temperature T only.
