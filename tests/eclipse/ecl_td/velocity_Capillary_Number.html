Capillary number

The capillary number is used in both the "Foam model" and the "Surfactant model". The capillary number represents the ratio of shear forces to capillary (surface tension) forces and is often defined as:

N c= upμp σpq [Eq. 739.1]

where:

u p is the velocity of phase p,
μ p is the viscosity of phase p,
σ pq is the surface tension between phases p and q.

The velocity across a cell face can be expressed in terms of the volume flow Q p which can in turn be expressed in terms of the transmissibility T and phase potential Pp:

up=Q p Am = CN CDTkr pΔPp Am μp [Eq. 739.2]

where:

C N is a unit conversion constant,
C D is the Darcy constant,
A m is the flow area, which could be A, A φ or AφSp depending on the velocity model, and
k rp is the relative permeability of phase p.

When equation 739.2 is substituted into equation 739.1, the capillary number (for a boundary face) is:

Ncn =CN CD T krpΔP p Am σp q=(k rp AA m )⋅(CN Kσ pq ∂P p∂n) [Eq. 739.3]

where:

A is the cell boundary area,
n is a direction, x, y or z.

The krp AAm term in equation 739.3 is often ignored:

N cn= CN CD TΔ Pp Aσpq = (CN Kσ pq ∂P p∂ n) [Eq. 739.4]

This is equivalent to using the Darcy velocity (A m=A), and assuming the flow is for a single phase ( krp =1). The cell capillary number is then determined from the boundary capillary numbers:

Nc=C N CD ‖ TΔP pA ‖1 σpq =C N‖K⋅∇Pp ‖σ pq [Eq. 739.5]

For two phases p and q there are two “natural” capillary numbers that could be used:

Ncpq =vpμp σpq

or:

Ncqp =vqμq σpq

ECLIPSE 100 capillary numbers

ECLIPSE 100 uses the second formulation, equation 739.5 for capillary numbers.

In the surfactant model, the oil phase potential is used together with the oil-water surface tension to determine the capillary number:

Ncow =C N ‖K⋅∇ Po ‖ σow [Eq. 739.6]

In the foam model, the oil phase potential is again used together with the water-gas surface tension:

Ncwg =C N ‖K⋅∇ Po ‖ σwg [Eq. 739.7]

In both these models an RMS value of ‖ K⋅∇Po ‖ is used, after the components have been averaged in a manner analogous to equation 736.1:

‖K⋅Δ P‖= (K x⋅ ∂P∂ x) 2+ (Ky⋅∂P∂y )2 +( Kz ⋅∂P ∂z )2 [Eq. 739.8]

which for a given cell i with neighbor j is calculated in the x direction according to:

Kx⋅∂P ∂x =0.5[ (Kx Dx ) i-1,  i ⋅(P i- Pi -1)+( Kx D x )i, i+1 ⋅(P i+1 -P i) ] [Eq. 739.9]

and similarly for the y and z directions. The K/ D value is calculated in an analogous manner to the transmissibility and depends upon how the geometry has been specified.

ECLIPSE 300 capillary numbers

ECLIPSE 300 uses the first formulation in equation 739.5 for capillary numbers.

In the surfactant model, the oil phase potential is used together with the oil-water surface tension:

Ncow = CN CD‖ TΔPo A‖ 1σow [Eq. 739.10]

In the foam model, the gas phase potential is used together with the water-gas surface tension:

Ncwg = CN CD‖ TΔPg A‖ 1σwg [Eq. 739.11]

The boundary terms are averaged in a manner analogous to equation 736.2 before the RMS value is calculated.

Matrix-fracture flows

In dual porosity simulations, the matrix-fracture flow contribution can be calculated in a similar manner to equations 739.10 and 739.11 but with the area term calculated by

A= Vσ [Eq. 739.12]

where V is the bulk volume of the cell and σ is the sigma factor representing the matrix block size. For fracture cells this contribution to the capillary number is likely to be small and will be ignored.
